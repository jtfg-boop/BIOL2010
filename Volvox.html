Volvox is a colonial green alga made up of hundreds to thousands of cells arranged in a hollow sphere, and it demonstrates one of the simplest examples of cell specialization in nature. Most of the cells are small, flagellated somatic cells that cover the colony's surface — each has two flagella and often a light-sensing eyespot, and together they beat in coordination to move the colony toward light for photosynthesis. These somatic cells cannot reproduce. A much smaller number of larger, non-flagellated cells called gonidia sit toward the interior of the colony; these are the only cells capable of dividing to produce new daughter colonies. This division between cells specialized for survival and movement versus cells specialized for reproduction is called germline-soma differentiation, and it makes Volvox an important model organism for studying how complex multicellular life evolved from single-celled ancestors.
