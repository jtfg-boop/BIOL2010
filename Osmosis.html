Osmosis — why water moves

Water molecules cross the membrane in both directions. The ones held in hydration shells around sucrose are not free to cross, so the side with more free water sends more molecules across — until the rising water column, or a cell wall, pushes back as hard as the free-water difference pulls.

The model counts molecules: a side sends water across in proportion to its free-water concentration plus the pressure on it; the hydration shell holds a fixed number of water molecules per sucrose. Molecules move by random (Brownian) motion, and only a molecule that happens to be near a pore can slip through it.
