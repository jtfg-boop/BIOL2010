Before you watch: osmosis is just diffusion, seen from water's point of view

1. Diffusion and concentration gradients. Molecules are always in random motion, bumping into each other and changing direction. If a substance is more concentrated in one region than another, that difference is called a concentration gradient. Because molecules move randomly, more of them will happen to wander from the crowded region into the less crowded one than the other way around, simply because there are more of them there to do the wandering. No molecule "knows" where to go; the net result just comes from the numbers. This net movement from high to low concentration is diffusion, and we say molecules move down their concentration gradient. It continues until the concentration is even everywhere. At that point molecules still move, but there is no net change.

2. Water diffuses too, but it's the free water that counts. Water molecules follow exactly the same rule. The question is: what is the "concentration" of water? Remember what dissolving means: each dissolved ion or polar molecule is surrounded by a hydration shell of water molecules, held in place by polar interactions such as hydrogen bonds. Those water molecules are hogged by the solute and aren't free to wander around. So the more solute a solution contains, the fewer free water molecules it has. Free water therefore moves down its own concentration gradient: from where there are more free water molecules (low solute concentration) to where there are fewer (high solute concentration).

Now picture a semi-permeable membrane, one that lets water through but not the solutes. The solutes can't cross to even things up, so the only thing that can move is water. The difference in solute concentration on the two sides determines the net direction of water movement: water moves toward the side with more solute. That is osmosis.
